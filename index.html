WordMapper

Five everyday words name any place on earth to one hexagon about 2.7 m across, and refuse a misheard one 98 % of the time. Nothing else is needed to decode them — no reference point, no network, no database.

Big Ben: one resolution-14 hexagon of Uber’s H3 grid, 1.5 m on an edge and 6.3 m² on average — smaller than a what3words square. Every word is graded CEFR A1–B2 — everyday English, nothing you would have to spell out.

Try it

Open the grid — click anywhere on earth and the address appears with its hexagon drawn round the point, or type five words to go to them. It is all computed in the page.

Five words from a 1,296-word list are 1,2965 ≈ 3.66 quadrillion combinations. H3 has 122 × 714 ≈ 82.7 trillion resolution-14 slots, which fits 44 times over, and the 44 becomes a check. Resolution 15 would fit only six times, for a hexagon smaller than anyone can point to.

H3 is there for shape. It lays hexagons on an icosahedron, so a cell is close to a regular hexagon everywhere on earth, and every neighbour is the same distance away. It is not equal-area: cells vary by about 2:1 across the globe.

Five words, one number

A word cannot hold four of H3’s base-7 digits (74 = 2,401), so one word per level would stop at resolution 13. Instead the five words are one number:

ordinal = base cell × 7¹⁴ + its fourteen digits in base 7
value   = ordinal × 44 + check         check = sha256(cell) mod 44
number  = scramble(value)              a permutation of all 1,296⁵
words   = number in base 1,296

A wrong word

A misheard word is refused about 98 % of the time: it fails the 1-in-44 check, lands past the last value that names a cell, or lands on a pentagon’s missing child.

The scramble makes the rest obvious. Without it, neighbouring places would share leading words, and a wrong last word would land next door. With it, changing any word sends the address somewhere unrelated. In the tests the median miss is about 9,700 km, and none landed within 100 km. It is a six-round Feistel network, and 1,2965 is 620, exactly 60,466,176², so it is a bijection on every five-word combination with nothing left over.

The price: neighbouring places share no words, and there is no shorter form — four words do not name a bigger area.

The word list

1,296 words, every one graded CEFR A1–B2 — the band a person can retrieve under pressure, not merely recognise. Generated by tools/wordlist from the 9,025 graded headwords of the Oxford 5000 and the English Vocabulary Profile, minus names, places, offensive and vulgar words, inflections of other words, words spelled two ways — and then everything confusable or sound-alike.

No two words are within one phoneme error of each other. Zero homophones, zero pairs one articulatory feature apart. The list is the first defence against a misheard word, the check the second, and the scramble the third.

BIP-39 was here first and was the wrong list. It is designed to be typed and checksummed, not spoken: it contains pair/pear, peace/piece, right/write and wear/where, and 53 % of its words have a same-or-one-phoneme twin.
