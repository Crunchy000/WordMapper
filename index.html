WordMapper

Three spoken words for any point on earth, to within a metre — by not saying the part you already know.

shoulder.few.lottery

Big Ben. The same three words repeat every 70 km; knowing roughly where you are picks the right one.

The trick

The world is projected to an equal-area plane and tiled into 70 km squares. The square is never transmitted. An address names a point within a square, and the listener supplies the square from knowing roughly where they are.

That omission is the whole scheme. 104,095 squares cover the earth, so leaving one unsaid is 16.7 bits you never speak aloud — and those bits go into resolution instead.

Bit budget

A BIP-39 word is exactly 11 bits, because the list is exactly 2,048 words. Nothing is wasted rounding to a word boundary.

The checksum is not optional

BIP-39 is built to be typed and checksummed, not spoken. Its guarantee is unique four-letter prefixes, which says nothing about sound. Measured across the list:

  • Four outright homophones — pair/pear, peace/piece, right/write, wear/where
  • 806 pairs one letter apart, 1,824 one phoneme apart
  • 53 % of words have a same-or-one-phoneme twin, so 89.6 % of three-word addresses contain a word that one mishearing turns into a different valid word

Without check bits, such a mishearing is silently a different place. Check bits come out of resolution, so this is the real trade:

Measured detection matches theory exactly. BIP-39 does get one thing right that cost real effort elsewhere: it contains zero plurals.

How good must the hint be?

Two instances of an address fall within R of a point exactly when the lattice spacing is below 2R. A 35 km guarantee therefore needs 70 km spacing in both directions — and an equal-area cylinder only delivers that at its standard parallel. The square is 70 km in projected units, not on the ground.

Fixes, none free: move the standard parallel to the region (55° makes it exactly square over the UK), UTM-style latitude bands, or an equal-area cube — which bounds the requirement near 27 km worldwide.

What it gives up

An address is no longer self-contained. shoulder.few.lottery means nothing to someone who has no idea where you are — which is precisely the emergency-services case that motivates schemes like this. The natural fix is a place-name prefix when out of context: “Cornwall: shoulder.few.lottery”.
