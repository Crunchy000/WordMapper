One address, any length

Every address is a prefix of a longer one. Each word narrows the area; none of them ever needs revising.

WordsBig BenArea
1plug22.0 km
2plug.curtain486 m — a street
3plug.curtain.elder10.7 m — a building
4plug.curtain.elder.script2.69 m — and verified

The root is a fixed box over the UK and Ireland rather than a repeating tile, so an address is unambiguous at every length. There are no repeats to disambiguate and no position hint is needed — knowing roughly where someone is becomes a sanity check, not a precondition.

The box is also the limit of coverage: outside it there is no address, and the codec refuses rather than inventing one. It has to. Decoding maps addresses onto the box and nowhere else, so an invented address would resolve to a real place inside it and pass its own checksum — the check covers the words, not where the caller was standing. A wider box would reach further at the cost of resolution everywhere: the whole world would put four words at 61 m rather than 2.7 m.

Which is the useful shape for an emergency call. A caller who manages two words before the line drops has still given a real answer; one who keeps going narrows it without going back on anything.

The part that is easy to get wrong

Coordinates are interleaved once at full precision and the bit string is truncated. Deriving the interleave order per length looks equivalent and is not: 11 bits per word is odd, so 33 bits splits the axes 17/16 while 22 and 44 split evenly. Those are three unrelated sequences.

When I built it that way the three-word address came out entirely different, while the two- and four-word ones agreed with each other — so a spot check on 2 and 4 would have passed. The test suite now checks every length over 4,000 points.

The fourth word does two jobs

Three words alone reach 10.75 m, which is wide. A whole fourth word of position would reach 24 cm, which is finer than anyone needs. So the fourth word's eleven bits are split between refinement and checksum:

RefineCheckCellWrong word caught
383.80 m99.61 %
472.69 m99.22 %
561.90 m98.44 %

Shipped at four refine plus seven check. For comparison, what3words is 3 m with no checksum at all — this is finer and verified.

A checksum genuinely cannot live at every length: its bits would sit where the next word's position bits must go, and reserving eight at every length would take two words from 486 m to 7.78 km. Putting it in the last word costs nothing at lengths one to three, which are simply unverified. Four words is therefore terminal.

The word list

The BIP-39 English list as published — 2,048 words, exactly 11 bits each, nothing wasted rounding to a word boundary.

It is designed to be typed and checksummed, not spoken. It contains pair/pear, peace/piece, right/write and wear/where, and 53 % of its words have a same-or-one-phoneme twin — so 89.6 % of three-word addresses contain a word that one mishearing turns into a different valid word. The check word is what stands against that, not the list.
