WordMapper

Four everyday words name any place on earth to one hexagon about the size of a house. Nothing else is needed to decode them — no reference point, no network, no database.

pattern.revolve.inch.partner

Big Ben: one resolution-12 hexagon of Uber’s H3 grid, 10.8 m on an edge, about 19 m across and 307 m² on average. Every word is graded CEFR A1–B2 — everyday English, nothing you would have to spell out.

Try it

Open the grid — click anywhere on earth and the address appears with its hexagon drawn round the point, or type four words to go to them. It is all computed in the page.

Why resolution 12

Four words from a 1,296-word list are 1,2964 ≈ 2.82 trillion combinations. H3 has 122 × 712 ≈ 1.69 trillion resolution-12 slots, which fits 1.67 times over; resolution 13 would need 11.8 trillion. Three words were tried first, and reach only resolution 8: a 0.74 km² hexagon, a whole village.

H3 is there for shape. It lays hexagons on an icosahedron, so a cell is close to a regular hexagon everywhere on earth, and every neighbour is the same distance away. It is not equal-area: resolution-12 cells vary by about 2:1 across the globe.

Four words, one number

A word cannot hold four of H3’s base-7 digits (74 = 2,401), so one word per level would stop at resolution 10. Instead the four words are one number:

ordinal = base cell × 7¹² + its twelve digits in base 7
number  = scramble(ordinal)            a permutation of all 1,296⁴
words   = number in base 1,296

A wrong word

1.67× is not a whole factor, so there is no room for a check digit. A misheard word is refused only when it lands past the last value that names a cell, or on a pentagon’s missing child: about 40 % of the time. The rest decode to a real place.

So the scramble makes those obvious. Without it, neighbouring places would share leading words, and a wrong last word would land next door. With it, changing any word sends the address somewhere unrelated. In the tests the median miss is about 10,000 km, and none of about 11,000 landed within 100 km. It is a six-round Feistel network, and 1,2964 is exactly 1,679,616², so it is a bijection on every four-word combination with nothing left over.

The price: neighbouring places share no words, and there is no shorter form — three words do not name a bigger area.

The word list

1,296 words, every one graded CEFR A1–B2 — the band a person can retrieve under pressure, not merely recognise. Generated by tools/wordlist from the 9,025 graded headwords of the Oxford 5000 and the English Vocabulary Profile, minus names, places, offensive and vulgar words, inflections of other words, words spelled two ways — and then everything confusable or sound-alike.

No two words are within one phoneme error of each other. Zero homophones, zero pairs one articulatory feature apart. With no check digit, the list is the first defence against a misheard word, and the scramble the second.

BIP-39 was here first and was the wrong list. It is designed to be typed and checksummed, not spoken: it contains pair/pear, peace/piece, right/write and wear/where, and 53 % of its words have a same-or-one-phoneme twin.
